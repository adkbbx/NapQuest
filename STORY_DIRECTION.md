# Proposed story: The Great Mossbrook Nap

This is a proposed content direction, not a description of newly implemented quests.

Mallow inherits a neglected woodland settlement and wants one uninterrupted afternoon nap. Each shortcut reveals another village problem. His solution is to build a village whose neighbours can earn, grow food and protect one another without him doing every chore.

The player can act directly or give Laya one goal. The central loop is: choose a village improvement, gather or earn its cost, build it, see it change life on the map, then face a new complication. Home levels mark village milestones rather than only larger resource bills.

| Home | Story milestone | Visible payoff | Complication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A place to call home | Clear a plot, repair the hollow, plant food | One very territorial goose steals the first lunch |
| 2 | Stop doing every chore | Wood and stone production, storage and paths | Productive stock attracts opportunistic raiders |
| 3 | A village needs neighbours | Kevin's trade requests, barracks and rabbit guards | The bees need help making their workplace sustainable |
| 4 | A place worth protecting | Walls, gates, rangers and stronger defenses | Coordinated raids test the layout and supply chain |
| 5 | The village takes a shift | Survive the Captain, then a celebration and Mallow's nap | Optional repeat raids and village improvement goals remain |

Kevin is a merchant and quartermaster: trade surplus, buy tools, obtain clear building advice. His requests teach the resource economy and reward useful village improvements.

The bees are neighbours with a production problem. Helping them establishes the honey supply; taking honey without agreement creates a temporary chase. Their following behaviour needs a visible reason, a time limit and a clear resolution. They should not follow indefinitely as the default state.

Use one goose identity consistently. The snack thief returns as the self-appointed Goose Captain, with increasingly ridiculous demands. Early comic encounters foreshadow later raids. Do not add a separate duck unless it has a distinct purpose.

The picnic becomes an optional treat or relationship event. Stealing it creates a chase; packing it can feed an expedition or settle a dispute. It is not a required detour for every village objective.

The bell becomes the village alarm. A muffler protects the bedroom at night; it should not disable the defenses. The quiet-bell episode is an optional introduction to crafting, not the reason all later goals exist.

Every quest needs a visible target, a short prerequisite list, a progress indicator, an on-map consequence and an explicit ending. New content should reuse the resource, building, trade and defense systems before introducing another disconnected currency or chore.
